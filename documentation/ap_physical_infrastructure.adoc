== Physical Infrastructure

=== Purpose

This document describes the real physical system controlled by the Aquaponics software.

It is intended to become the authoritative physical-system reference.

Unlike the architecture documents, this document may change as the actual installation changes.

=== Physical System Overview

The aquaponics installation contains:

* fish;
* water;
* plant-growing areas;
* circulation;
* aeration;
* lighting;
* electrical control;
* sensors;
* supporting infrastructure.

The software exists to control and observe this physical installation.

=== Biological System

==== Fish Tanks / Pools

The system contains one or more water bodies used to keep fish.

For each production installation, document:

* identifier;
* dimensions;
* approximate volume;
* fish species;
* approximate stocking;
* connection to water circuit;
* overflow behaviour;
* aeration;
* sensors.

==== Grow Beds

Grow beds provide:

* plant-growing space;
* water treatment;
* biological filtration.

For each bed, document:

* identifier;
* dimensions;
* media;
* plant type;
* water inlet;
* water outlet;
* flood/drain or circulation behaviour.

=== Water Circuit

The water circuit is a first-class physical subsystem.

Document:

* pump;
* pipe routes;
* diameters;
* valves;
* split points;
* return paths;
* overflow paths;
* refill points;
* drains;
* failure behaviour.

A diagram should eventually identify every major water path.

Failure analysis of the water circuit should include where applicable:

* pump stopped;
* loss of electrical power;
* blocked inlet;
* blocked return;
* disconnected pipe;
* leaking connection;
* unintended siphoning;
* overflow;
* dry-running pump;
* loss of prime.

The physical system should avoid turning a single ordinary failure into uncontrolled drainage of the fish tank or flooding of electrical equipment.

=== Relay System

The controller operates an 8-channel relay system.

The relay electronics are currently powered from the Raspberry Pi power domain.

This arrangement has operated successfully and is considered acceptable unless measured power requirements indicate otherwise.

The relay board controls physical mains-voltage devices.

====
GPIO-level control and mains-voltage switching are different electrical domains.

All mains-voltage wiring must use appropriate electrical safety practices.

=== Temperature Sensing

The Sense HAT onboard temperature measurement is not considered suitable for environmental measurement because Raspberry processor heat distorts the reading.

The new design shall use dedicated external sensors.

A DS18B20-type external temperature sensor has already been considered and physically identified for use.

Production documentation shall identify:

* sensor ID;
* physical location;
* connection;
* calibration;
* acceptable range;
* failure handling.

=== Sense HAT

The Sense HAT was used by the earlier controller for:

* display;
* clock/status visualization;
* onboard measurements.

The new controller does not require the Sense HAT.

Its removal simplifies:

* GPIO access;
* physical construction;
* sensor attachment;
* system size;
* dependency structure.

The Sense HAT shall therefore not be considered a required component of the new production architecture.

=== Flow

Flow sensing may later provide stronger confirmation that commanded pumping produces real circulation.

This would allow the system to distinguish:

[source,text]
----
pump command = ON
----

from:

[source,text]
----
water flow confirmed
----

No flow sensor is currently required.

=== Camera

Camera functionality is optional.

During development, a Raspberry Pi native camera may be used because it is simple to integrate.

The permanent camera architecture may instead use:

* wired IP camera;
* PoE camera;
* dedicated camera node.

The permanent camera location should be determined by:

* view;
* fish visibility;
* reflections;
* lighting;
* maintenance;
* environmental protection.

The Raspberry controller location should not be dictated solely by camera cable length.

=== Camera Networking

A wired IP camera does not need to connect directly to the Raspberry.

Both devices may connect to the same Ethernet switch.

Conceptually:

[source,text]
----
              Ethernet Switch
                /        \
               /          \
       Raspberry          Camera
----

No wireless connection is required.

=== PoE

Power over Ethernet may be used for suitable remote network devices.

PoE can carry:

* Ethernet data;
* electrical power;

over one network cable.

PoE may be especially useful for cameras positioned far from the controller.

=== Wired Communication

The physical production installation shall prefer wired connectivity.

Default rule:

* no Wi-Fi dependency;
* no Bluetooth dependency.

Suitable technologies include:

* Ethernet;
* 1-Wire;
* GPIO;
* I2C;
* SPI;
* USB;
* serial communication.

=== Controller Power Domain

Low-power controller-attached devices may be powered from the Raspberry where appropriate.

Examples:

* relay electronics;
* DS18B20;
* similar low-power sensors.

The actual power budget shall be measured on the final controller.

=== SSD Power

An external SSD may draw substantially more current than simple sensors.

The SSD may therefore use an independent power source while retaining USB data connectivity to the Raspberry.

The final SSD/controller combination shall be tested as one hardware set.

=== Power Supply

The controller requires a stable power source.

The production hardware design shall consider:

* normal load;
* peak load;
* boot load;
* all relay channels active;
* storage activity;
* attached peripherals;
* undervoltage;
* cable voltage drop.

Power margin shall be based on realistic peak conditions rather than only idle consumption.

=== UPS

A UPS or equivalent battery-backed solution should eventually protect the controller.

Its purposes may include:

* surviving short outages;
* detecting prolonged outage;
* allowing controlled shutdown;
* preventing repeated rapid power cycling.

The UPS is part of controller durability, not a replacement for safe physical design.

=== Controller Enclosure and Location

The production controller shall be installed in a location suitable for long-term unattended operation.

Document:

* controller mounting position;
* enclosure;
* ventilation;
* ambient-temperature range;
* humidity exposure;
* condensation risk;
* splash/water exposure;
* cable entry;
* strain relief;
* service access;
* separation from mains wiring;
* separation from plumbing where practical.

The Raspberry Pi, storage, relay-control electronics, and associated connectors shall not remain unnecessarily exposed to the humid aquaponics environment.

Cooling and enclosure design shall avoid creating a condensation problem while solving a temperature problem.

=== Physical Fail-Safe Behaviour

For every actuator the installation shall answer:

[IMPORTANT]
====
What happens physically if the controller disappears completely?
====

Possible physical defaults include:

* OFF;
* ON;
* valve open;
* valve closed;
* mechanically unchanged.

There is no universal rule that "everything OFF" is safest.

The correct state depends on the physical subsystem.

=== Boot-State Behaviour

GPIO and relays may pass through temporary states during:

* power-on;
* Linux boot;
* GPIO initialization;
* Engine startup;
* shutdown.

Critical outputs shall therefore be tested through the complete boot and shutdown sequence.

The production design shall document for each critical GPIO-controlled output:

* GPIO pull-up/pull-down state;
* relay-board input polarity;
* relay state before GPIO initialization;
* relay state if the GPIO becomes high-impedance;
* physical device state if Raspberry power disappears.

Where necessary, hardware pull resistors or equivalent electrical measures shall establish a deterministic state independently of application software.

=== Mains Safety

The system controls mains-voltage devices.

Physical implementation shall address:

* insulation;
* enclosures;
* grounding;
* cable rating;
* relay rating;
* fusing;
* moisture;
* strain relief;
* separation of low-voltage and mains wiring.

Software cannot compensate for unsafe mains wiring.

The installation shall also address:

* residual-current protection (RCD/GFCI equivalent);
* appropriate circuit protection;
* splash and condensation protection;
* suitable enclosure ingress protection;
* drip loops and cable routing;
* separation between wet plumbing paths and electrical equipment;
* connector protection;
* corrosion;
* safe maintenance isolation;
* compliance with applicable electrical regulations.

Mains-voltage equipment shall be positioned and enclosed so that an ordinary plumbing leak does not directly expose energized conductors.
