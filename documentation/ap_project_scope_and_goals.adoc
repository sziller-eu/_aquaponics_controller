== Project Scope and Goals

=== Purpose

The Aquaponics project develops and documents a physically and logically scalable aquaponic system for producing:

* fish;
* vegetables;
* herbs;
* other suitable plants.

The project covers both:

* the physical aquaponics installation;
* the software and technical infrastructure required to control, observe, maintain, and extend it.

The system is intended to be a real, continuously operating physical installation.

=== Ownership and Control

The physical installation and the software controlling it shall remain under the operator's control.

The software shall be open source.

The project shall minimize dependencies that could prevent future:

* operation;
* maintenance;
* repair;
* modification;
* deployment;
* extension;
* migration to replacement hardware.

[IMPORTANT]
====
"Free" in the context of this project does not primarily mean "free of charge".

The project aims for technological and operational independence.
====

Preference shall therefore be given to:

* decentralization;
* open-source software;
* open protocols;
* documented interfaces;
* locally operable systems;
* standard Linux infrastructure;
* replaceable hardware;
* reproducible deployments;
* data formats that remain accessible without a vendor service.

Dependencies that create avoidable external control over the system should be avoided.

Examples include mandatory:

* proprietary cloud services;
* online licensing;
* external authentication services;
* vendor accounts;
* subscriptions;
* closed remote-control platforms;
* inaccessible proprietary data formats.

A proprietary component may still be used when technically justified, provided that it does not become an unnecessary
single point of dependency for the complete installation.

=== Fundamental Operating Goal

The Aquaponics system shall be able to keep the physical installation operational without continuous human supervision.

The lowest-level controller shall be capable of operating:

* continuously;
* unattended;
* locally;
* without Internet access;
* without a user interface;
* without an AI agent;
* without an external application;
* without SHMC availability;
* without cloud services.

External systems may observe or influence the controller, but they shall not be required for normal physical operation.

=== Physical Scalability

The physical system shall be modular and extendable.

Its major physical domains include the following.

==== Priority 1 - Biological and Water System

* fish tanks or pools;
* fish;
* grow beds and equivalent plant containers;
* vegetables;
* herbs;
* other plants;
* local water circulation;
* pumps;
* immediate piping;
* overflow and return paths;
* biological filtration functions.

==== Priority 2 - Immediate Technical Infrastructure

* electrical wiring required by the aquaponics installation;
* sensors;
* actuators;
* relays;
* local control electronics;
* local water-management devices.

==== Priority 3 - Housing and Environmental Infrastructure

The installation may operate inside a greenhouse or similar enclosure.

Possible systems include:

* lighting;
* ventilation;
* heating;
* cooling;
* water refill;
* windows;
* shading;
* large-scale piping;
* large-scale electrical distribution.

==== Priority 4 - Independent Energy Infrastructure

Later installations may include:

* photovoltaic generation;
* battery storage;
* backup power;
* energy monitoring;
* energy-management systems.

The priority numbering expresses approximate physical and operational importance rather than a mandatory development sequence.

=== Logical Scalability

The software architecture shall allow the system to grow without requiring a redesign of the complete controller.

Future extensions may include:

* additional tanks;
* additional grow beds;
* additional pumps;
* additional lighting zones;
* additional temperature sensors;
* pH sensing;
* redox sensing;
* water-level sensing;
* flow sensing;
* automated feeding;
* automatic refill;
* ventilation;
* heating;
* shading;
* energy monitoring;
* cameras;
* vision analysis;
* remote applications;
* AI-assisted management.

Adding a new device or subsystem should normally require:

* a defined interface;
* an implementation;
* configuration;
* domain integration where required;

rather than changes throughout unrelated parts of the codebase.

=== Production Controller

The primary software component is the Aquaponics Engine.

The Engine is the autonomous manager of the physical aquaponics installation.

[IMPORTANT]
====
The API is not the manager.

The browser frontend is not the manager.

The mobile application is not the manager.

SHMC is not the manager.

An AI agent is not the manager.

The Aquaponics Engine is the manager.
====

The production Engine shall:

* observe the physical installation;
* maintain its logical state;
* execute schedules;
* make normal low-level control decisions;
* accept and execute authorized state-changing requests through its authoritative command mechanism;
* operate physical devices;
* persist relevant state;
* record history;
* detect degraded or failed components;
* recover automatically where appropriate.

=== External Management

Higher-level systems may interact with the Engine.

Possible external management actors and clients include:

* a human operator;
* the local web frontend;
* the mobile application;
* SHMC;
* an AI agent;
* future automation systems.

These systems interact through defined interfaces.

They may:

* observe;
* inspect history;
* request actions;
* configure supported parameters;
* activate authorized overrides.

They shall not bypass the Engine and manipulate physical hardware directly.

State-changing requests remain subject to Engine authority and shall enter the Engine through its supported command path.

=== Production and Experimental Separation

The production Aquaponics installation shall not be responsible for running:

* Digital Twin experiments;
* evolutionary optimization;
* physical simulation;
* experimental historical replay;
* arbitrary research workloads.

The production runtime exists to control the real physical installation.

However, the Engine shall have sufficiently clean architectural boundaries that the same Engine implementation may later be instantiated elsewhere with alternative:

* input sources;
* output adapters;
* clocks;
* persistence implementations;
* configuration;
* runtime environments.

This requirement exists to preserve reusability.

It does not make simulation part of the production Aquaponics deployment.

[IMPORTANT]
====
Production-only runtime, reusable Engine architecture.
====

=== Connectivity Philosophy

Permanent infrastructure should prefer physical wired connections.

The intended production philosophy is:

* wired Ethernet for network infrastructure;
* physical buses for local hardware;
* no Wi-Fi dependency;
* no Bluetooth dependency.

Loss of network connectivity shall not stop the local controller from performing its essential physical responsibilities.

=== Project Boundaries

The project includes:

* the Aquaponics Engine;
* hardware-access layers;
* persistence;
* API;
* administrative CLI;
* local browser frontend;
* deployment tooling;
* production runtime infrastructure;
* documentation;
* mobile remote-control application in a separate software unit.

The project does not require all optional subsystems to exist before the core controller is considered usable.

The minimum useful system is an autonomous physical controller capable of safely operating the essential aquaponics installation.

=== Design Priority

When design goals conflict, the approximate priority order is:

. safety of people;
. survival and welfare of the biological system;
. understandable and deterministic control behavior;
. reliability;
. recoverability;
. autonomy;
. observability;
. maintainability;
. architectural simplicity;
. modularity;
. extensibility;
. performance;
. visual appearance;
. convenience.

[IMPORTANT]
====
The Aquaponics project is first a physical life-support controller.

Convenience features must not reduce the reliability of the core physical system.
====
